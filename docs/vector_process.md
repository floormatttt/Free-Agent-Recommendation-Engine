# Vector Process

Covers `build_playtype_vectors.py`, `build_defense_vectors.py`, and `build_player_vectors.py` -- the pipeline that turns raw player stats into the finalized 12-category player vector (player.md, defense.md).

Output: `data/player_vectors_json/player_vectors_<season>.json`, one file per season, keyed by `PLAYER_ID`, each entry `{PLAYER_NAME, TEAM, SEASON, VECTOR}` where `VECTOR` is a 12-key category -> score map. Currently generated for 2018-19 through 2025-26 (8 seasons -- see Changelog for why 2016-17/2017-18 were dropped).

---

## Shared machinery: experience-adjusted time decay

Both `build_playtype_vectors.py` and `build_defense_vectors.py` blend up to 3 trailing seasons (current, current-1, current-2) using the same decay scheme:

- `experience` = years since draft (`DRAFT_YEAR`), or number of seasons on file as a fallback for undrafted players.
- `decay_strength(experience)`: U-shaped in experience -- high at the start and end of a career (recent form dominates), low ("bottoms out") at the prime (`PRIME_CENTER=7`, `PRIME_SPREAD=5`, `DECAY_MIN=0.15`, `DECAY_MAX=1.2`).
- Weights for available seasons: `exp(-decay_strength * i)` for `i` in `{0, 1, 2}` (0 = current), normalized over whichever seasons actually exist in `data/player_stats/`.
- A missing trailing season (rookie, or simply outside our data window) is left out of the weighting and the remaining weights renormalize -- no imputation, no distinction between "missed due to injury" and "missing because our dataset doesn't go back further." Verified directly: in 2016-17 (earliest season on file), every player's decayed value equals their raw single-season value exactly (only `i=0` available); in 2017-18, values are genuine 2-season blends.

## Playtype scores (`build_playtype_vectors.py`)

For each of the 9 play types (Isolation, PNR Handler, PNR Roll Man, Post Up, Spot Up, Handoff, Cut, Off Screen, Put Backs):

1. Decay-blend `FREQ%` and `PPP` across up to 3 seasons (see above).
2. **Baseline substitution:** if a season has no recorded data for a play type (player never ran it), substitute `BASELINE_FREQ=0.005`, `BASELINE_PPP=0.9` for that season before blending, per player.md.
3. **Score conversion:** player.md specifies `z(value) - z(0)` rather than a plain z-score. Since `z(value) - z(0) = (value-mean)/std - (0-mean)/std = value/std`, this simplifies to just `value / population_std` -- a player who never runs a play type scores exactly 0, not a large negative z-score. Applied to both FREQ and PPP.
4. **Playtype score:** geometric mean of the FREQ-score and PPP-score (`sqrt(freq_score * ppp_score)`).

Output columns: `{TYPE}_FREQ_DECAYED`, `{TYPE}_PPP_DECAYED`, `{TYPE}_FREQ_SCORE`, `{TYPE}_PPP_SCORE`, `{TYPE}_SCORE` (the final playtype score used in the 12-category vector).

## Defense & rebounding scores (`build_defense_vectors.py`)

Implements defense.md's 3 blocks. Same decay-blend machinery as playtypes; missing raw stats (no baseline rule defined for defense) fall back to that season's population mean as a neutral stand-in.

### Perimeter defense block

1. Rate-adjust: `contested_3pt_pg = CONTESTED_SHOTS_3PT / GP`, `deflections_pg = DEFLECTIONS / GP`.
2. **Compute Defensive Win Shares via Dean Oliver's formula, then convert to a per-game rate** (replaced both the earlier on-court-DRTG-shrunk-toward-team-DRTG approach and a brief intermediate approach that used NBA.com's own `DEF_WS` field directly -- see Changelog): `scripts/compute_dws.py` computes `DWS_RAW = ((MP_player/MP_team) * Poss_def_team * (1.08*PPP_league - DRtg_player/100)) / (0.32 * PPG_league * (Pace_team/Pace_league))` per player-season using `DEF_RATING` (measure_type="Advanced") as `DRtg_player`, then `dws_per_game = DWS_RAW / GP`. Saved directly onto `data/player_stats/*.csv` as `DWS_PER_GAME`, so `build_defense_vectors.py`'s `load_season()` just reads it rather than deriving it.
3. Zero-anchored z-scores (`value/std`, no mean subtraction -- these raw stats are naturally non-negative, so this stays non-negative) for `contested_3pt_pg`, `deflections_pg`, `STL_PCT`.
4. `activity_score = contested_3pt_z^0.35 * deflections_z^0.35 * stl_pct_z^0.30`.
5. `adjusted_dws = dws_per_game + (0.20 * activity_score * std(dws_per_game))`.
6. `adjusted_dws_z = (adjusted_dws - mean(adjusted_dws)) / std(adjusted_dws)`.
7. `perimeter_d_block = adjusted_dws_z - min(adjusted_dws_z)` (shifted so the worst player = 0).
8. **Rescaled to match RIM_BLOCK/REB_BLOCK's magnitude** (see Changelog) -- the min-shift in step 7 systematically inflated the mean relative to the other two blocks.

### Rim protection block

1. Zero-anchored z-scores for `BLK%` (rate, always non-negative), `DFG%` (always non-negative), `Diff%` (can be negative), `DFGA` (always non-negative).
2. Invert `DFG%_z` and `Diff%_z` via max-shift (`max(z) - z`) so higher = better and both stay non-negative regardless of sign -- this is the fix for a sign-flip bug found in an earlier version (see Changelog).
3. `rim_block = BLK%_z^0.25 * DFG%_z_inv^0.30 * Diff%_z_inv^0.35 * DFGA_z^0.10`. All 4 factors are non-negative by construction, so ordinary exponentiation is safe (no signed-power trick needed).

### Rebounding block

1. Rate-adjust: `def_box_outs_pg = DEF_BOXOUTS / GP`.
2. Zero-anchored z-scores for `DREB%`, `OREB%`, `def_box_outs_pg` (all non-negative).
3. `reb_block = (DREB%_z * OREB%_z * def_box_outs_pg_z) ^ (1/3)`.

## Final assembly (`build_player_vectors.py`)

Merges the 9 `{TYPE}_SCORE` columns from the playtype CSV with `RIM_BLOCK -> RIM_DEFENSE`, `PERIMETER_D_BLOCK -> PERIMETER_DEFENSE`, `REB_BLOCK -> REBOUNDING` from the defense CSV, into the final 12-key `VECTOR` per player, saved as JSON.

## Team vectors (`team.md`, `build_team_vectors.py`)

Same 12 categories as the player vector, one **need** vector per team, but computed **directly from team-level stats** (`data/team_stats/team_stats_<season>.csv`) -- not aggregated from player vectors. `team.md`'s design went through several pivots on this point (see Changelog): the original plan was a minutes-weighted average of player vectors with a corrective rescale, but computing straight from team-level numbers sidesteps the problem that correction existed to fix; later, the output itself was flipped from a strength vector (higher = better, same sense as the player vector) to a need vector (0-1, 0 = approaching best-ever/no need, 1 = approaching worst-ever/nonexistent, via a sigmoid rather than min-max); most recently, the normalization reference moved from the full 8-season `STANDARD_SEASONS` pool to a 3-season rolling window (that season + 2 trailing), so "best/worst ever" tracks how the league is actually playing recently rather than blending across playstyle eras.

- **Output:** `data/team_vectors_json/team_vectors_<season>.json`, 30 teams keyed by abbreviation, each entry's `NEED_VECTOR` holding the 12-category `[0,1]` need scores (see team.md's "Need-score conversion").
- **Playtypes (9 categories):** single-season (no decay blend -- see below), but **structurally different from the player pipeline**, not just frequency-inverted: `need_raw = percentile_rank(FREQ_PCT) * (1 - percentile_rank(PPP)) ** EFFECTIVENESS_GATE`, `score = 1 - need_raw`, `EFFECTIVENESS_GATE = 1` (production default -- see Changelog for the `k=1` vs `k=2`-`k=4` comparison that landed here). A *gated interaction*, not a weighted geometric mean of two z-scores -- a team with the literal best effectiveness in the league gets a full pass on frequency regardless of how extreme it is, since `ppp_badness = 0` zeroes out `need_raw`. Unlike the player-side formula (where high frequency + high effectiveness together mean a notable skill), team need is about exposure: high frequency + low effectiveness is the worst quadrant (heavy reliance on something the team is bad at), low frequency + high effectiveness is the best (not a live problem) -- see team.md's "Playtype scores" section for why the earlier weighted-geometric-mean version (kept as z-scored `freq_z_inv^a * ppp_z^b`) didn't actually achieve this and was replaced.
- **Perimeter defense, rim protection, rebounding (3 categories):** the same 3-block formulas as defense.md, with two substitutions where `team_stats` has no direct player-level analog on file: `STL_PG`/`BLK_PG` (per-game, not opponent-shot-normalized rates like the player-level `PCT_STEALS`/`PCT_BLOCKS`) stand in for those two blocks' rate inputs, and `RIM_DFGA` (a season total on file) is divided by `GP` before use. `RIM_DFG_PCT`/`RIM_DIFF_PCT`/`OREB_PCT`/`DREB_PCT` are already team-level rates and used as-is. Same `PERIMETER_D_BLOCK` mean-inflation rescale as the player pipeline is applied here too.
- **No empirical-Bayes shrinkage** on `DEF_RATING` (unlike the player-level on-court DRTG): a team's season `DEF_RATING` is already a full-sample outcome, not a noisy few-possession on/off split, so there's nothing to shrink toward.
- **No multi-season decay:** a team's roster/scheme can turn over completely year to year (coaching change, core trade), so blending across seasons risks mixing genuinely different rosters rather than smoothing noise -- deliberate omission, not a gap.
- **Known limitations (from team.md):** `STL_PG`/`BLK_PG` are per-game counts, not true rate stats (no team-level opponent-possession-normalized steal/block rate exists on file; a per-100-possessions version using the Advanced endpoint's `POSS` column would be more rigorous); `RIM_DFGA` being a season total means dividing by `GP` matters more when teams' games-played differ mid-season; no team-level analog of `EXPERIENCE_YRS`-based decay (deliberate, per the point above).

Sourced `DRtg_player` from `DEF_RATING` via `LeagueDashPlayerStats(measure_type="Advanced")` rather than `measure_type="Defense")` (which is what the immediately preceding entry's `DEF_WS` pull used) -- confirmed the `Advanced` measure type has zero gaps across all 12 seasons on file, including the 5 that `Defense` returned completely empty for, so this incidentally fixed that limitation too. Added `POSS`/`PACE` (team Advanced) and `PTS`/`MIN` (team Base) to `pull_nba_data.py`'s team pull and backfilled them plus player `DEF_RATING` into all 12 existing seasons via `scripts/backfill_dws_inputs.py` (replacing the now-obsolete `backfill_def_ws.py`, which was deleted). **Caught and fixed a real bug before it reached production**: initially used the team_stats `MIN` column directly as `MP_team`, which produced `DWS_RAW` values up to `25` (real-world Basketball-Reference DWS tops out around `5`-`6`) -- diagnosed as `MIN` being the team's own single-unit game minutes (~48/game, ~3966 total for an 82-game season) rather than the roster's combined 5-on-court minutes (~19680) the formula expects; fixed by computing `MP_team` as the sum of that team's own players' `MIN` from player_stats instead. Verified post-fix against real-world results: 2023-24's computed leaderboard is topped by Rudy Gobert at `DWS_RAW≈5.24`, matching Basketball-Reference's actual leader and value closely, with Minnesota players dominating the next several spots (consistent with the Timberwolves' real #1 defensive rating that season). Updated `build_defense_vectors.py`'s `load_season()` to read the pre-computed `DWS_PER_GAME` column directly (renamed from the prior entry's `DEF_WS_PER48`) instead of deriving a rate itself; `ALL_STAT_COLS`, `ADJUSTED_DWS_PER_GAME`, and related variable names updated to match. Regenerated all 8 standard seasons' defense vectors and final player vectors. Verified 0 `NaN`s in `PERIMETER_D_BLOCK` for 2025-26 (582/582 players), and the leaderboard's low-minute-noise problem (the immediately preceding entry's known limitation) is markedly improved, though not eliminated -- e.g. Zach Edey (11 GP) still tops the 2025-26 leaderboard and a couple of sub-100-minute players score unrealistically high, but the bulk of the top 15 are now legitimate rotation defenders (SGA, Wembanyama, Holmgren, Derrick White, Amen Thompson) rather than the prior entry's near-total garbage-time domination. New known limitations (see defense.md): remaining low-minute noise (no shrinkage step), `DEF_RATING` still being team-influenced (Oliver-style, not isolated), `Poss_def_team` approximated by a team's own offensive-tempo `POSS` rather than a true defensive-possession count, and a traded player's team-level inputs coming from only their most recent team.
